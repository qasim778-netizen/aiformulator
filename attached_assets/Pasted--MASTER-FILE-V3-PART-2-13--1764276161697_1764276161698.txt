======================================================================
MASTER FILE V3 — PART 2
13-SECTION PUBLIC PAGE LAYOUT (ALL CATEGORIES)
======================================================================

After <h2>Page Strategy</h2>, you MUST generate the following
13 sections in this exact order.

Each section:
• Uses <h2> for heading
• Contains one or more <p> or <ul>/<ol> blocks
• Paragraphs are 4–7 sentences
• Content length per section ±120–200 words
• Tone and examples must adapt to the detected category group

======================================================================
SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
======================================================================

<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>
Provide a clear, long-form introduction to the product, explaining what it is,
what type of formulation it represents, and the main purpose it serves.
Mention the kind of users or industries that typically use it and the key
performance idea behind the product. Adapt the tone to the category: gentle
for baby and pet care, premium for beauty, technical for industrial, and
practical for cleaning or car care.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 2 — PROBLEMS THIS PRODUCT SOLVES
======================================================================

<h2>Problems This Product Solves</h2>
<p>
Describe the real-world problems, frustrations, or operational challenges that
this formulation is meant to resolve. For example, stains that are difficult
to remove, odors that linger, surfaces that degrade over time, or production
issues that need stable chemistry. Use 2–3 mini-scenarios showing before/after
situations so potential users clearly understand why this product exists and
what gap it fills.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 3 — KEY BENEFITS
======================================================================

<h2>Key Benefits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Explain a primary benefit related to visible results or performance.</li>
<li>Describe a second benefit related to ease of use, process efficiency, or
user experience.</li>
<li>Describe a third benefit related to durability, protection, or long-term
value.</li>
<li>Add one more benefit if relevant, focused on category-specific strengths
(e.g., gentleness, safety, environmental impact, or technical reliability).</li>
</ul>
<p>
After the bullet list, add a short paragraph summarizing how these benefits
work together to make the formulation attractive for its intended users.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 4 — HOW IT WORKS
======================================================================

<h2>How It Works</h2>
<p>
Explain the functional or scientific mechanism behind the formulation in a
non-academic but informed way. Describe how active components, surfactants,
polymers, resins, conditioners, chelating agents, or other functional
ingredients interact with surfaces, soils, fibers, or substrates. The
explanation must be understandable to a non-specialist but still feel
technically credible. Align the description with the detected category,
e.g., fiber interaction for fabric care, film formation for coatings, or
surface energy changes for adhesives.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 5 — INGREDIENT FUNCTIONS
======================================================================

<h2>Ingredient Functions</h2>
<p>
Break the formulation into functional ingredient groups and briefly explain
what each group does, without using percentages. For example, describe
cleaning agents, solvents, emulsifiers, rheology modifiers, anti-corrosion
additives, conditioners, emollients, or fragrance components. Highlight how
these groups support the overall performance and stability of the product.
Make this section feel like a conceptual map of the formulation, not a
detailed recipe.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 6 — PERFORMANCE ADVANTAGES
======================================================================

<h2>Performance Advantages</h2>
<p>
Explain what makes this formulation stand out compared to generic or simple
solutions. Discuss aspects such as faster action, better compatibility,
reduced residue, enhanced gloss, improved durability, safer profile, more
consistent processing, or better end-user experience. Adapt the emphasis to
the specific category: shine and protection for car care, mildness and
sensory qualities for beauty, robust adhesion or curing behavior for
construction, material science reliability for industrial systems.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 7 — APPLICATION INSTRUCTIONS
======================================================================

<h2>Application Instructions</h2>
<ol>
<li>
Describe how the user should prepare the product and the surface, substrate,
or fabric before use. Mention any dilution, mixing, or inspection required.
</li>
<li>
Explain the correct way to apply the product step-by-step, including tools
(sponge, sprayer, cloth, brush, applicator, printing nozzle, etc.) and
recommended contact time or passes.
</li>
<li>
Describe how to complete the process: rinsing, wiping, curing, drying,
buffing, or post-treatment care. Mention what a successful result should
look or feel like after correct use.
</li>
</ol>
<p>
Emphasize any category-specific nuances such as low mechanical action for
delicate fibers, even film thickness for coatings, or correct joint filling
for adhesives.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 8 — SURFACE / MATERIAL COMPATIBILITY
======================================================================

<h2>Surface / Material Compatibility</h2>
<p>
List and describe the main surfaces, materials, or substrates that are well
suited for this formulation. Specify where the product provides optimal
performance, such as textiles, leather, painted metal, plastics, stone,
cement, resin-printed parts, or specific industrial materials. Also mention
any surfaces or conditions where caution is recommended or where testing is
advised before full-scale use. This section should help users quickly judge
whether the product fits their use case.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 9 — PRODUCT TYPES / VARIANTS
======================================================================

<h2>Product Types / Variants</h2>
<p>
Explain possible variants of this formulation that a manufacturer could
develop from the same core technology. For example: ready-to-use spray vs
concentrate, low-foam vs high-foam version, fragrance options, color
options, or packaging formats targeted at professional vs household users.
Make it clear that the core formulation concept can be tuned for different
markets and application methods without revealing percentages.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 10 — INDUSTRY APPLICATIONS
======================================================================

<h2>Industry Applications</h2>
<p>
Describe which industries, sectors, or professional environments can benefit
from this formulation. For instance, list textile care, automotive detailing,
household cleaning, institutional hygiene, construction, industrial
maintenance, 3D printing service bureaus, agriculture, or pet grooming
salons, depending on the product category. For each example, briefly explain
why the formulation is a good fit and what operational advantage it offers.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 11 — SAFETY NOTES
======================================================================

<h2>Safety Notes</h2>
<p>
Provide general safety guidance appropriate for the category. Mention
practical precautions such as wearing gloves or eye protection when needed,
keeping the product away from children and pets, avoiding ingestion, and
not mixing with incompatible chemicals (for example, oxidizing agents or
bleach for some cleaning products). Keep the tone responsible but not
fear-inducing, and avoid any regulatory or medical claims. The aim is to
reinforce responsible, professional handling.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 12 — STORAGE & STABILITY
======================================================================

<h2>Storage & Stability</h2>
<p>
Explain how to store the product to maintain performance and shelf life.
Mention preferred temperature ranges, protection from direct sunlight, and
the importance of keeping containers tightly sealed. Provide a typical
qualitative shelf-life expectation (e.g., many months under recommended
conditions) without promising precise dates. If relevant, note any
sensitivity to freezing, excessive heat, or moisture.
</p>

======================================================================
SECTION 13 — FAQS (MINIMUM 3)
======================================================================

<h2>FAQs</h2>
<p>
<strong>Q:</strong> Ask a common practical question about how or where to use the product.<br>
<strong>A:</strong> Provide a clear, helpful answer tailored to the category.
</p>
<p>
<strong>Q:</strong> Ask a second question about compatibility, safety, or performance expectations.<br>
<strong>A:</strong> Give a reassuring, realistic answer with actionable advice.
</p>
<p>
<strong>Q:</strong> Ask a third question about storage, re-use, or troubleshooting if the first result was not satisfactory.<br>
<strong>A:</strong> Provide guidance about adjustment in use conditions or when to re-test on a small area.
</p>
<p>
Optionally, you may add a fourth FAQ where helpful, as long as the question
and answer remain unique and relevant to this specific product category.
</p>
